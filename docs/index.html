A note on the dates. Real Portuguese demand is lowest at weekends and at night, and dips on public holidays. In this file the quietest days are Tuesday and Wednesday, the lowest hour is 13:00, and no holiday dip shows up at any date shift we tried. The dates and hours in the dataset therefore look offset from the real calendar (we could not find out why). The weekly and daily cycles are intact, so the comparison of models holds, but read the names of days and hours as labels, not as real weekdays.

5. What these models are, in plain words

Naive forecasts
The baselines. "Tomorrow equals today", or "tomorrow equals the same weekday last week". Any serious model has to beat them.
ARIMA
A recipe that predicts the next value from the most recent values and the most recent mistakes. It has no notion of weekdays.
SARIMA
ARIMA plus a weekly cycle. Adding the weekly rhythm was what made the difference in this project.
KNN (k-nearest neighbours)
Looks for the most similar past stretches (the last seven days) and averages what happened next.
Random Forest
Many small decision trees that vote. In the last version we also told it the position of the day in the weekly cycle.
AIC
A score that rewards a model for fitting the data and penalises it for being complicated. Lower is better. The weekly term took it from to .

Limits worth knowing

  • One test period of about five months, from . The close models (SARIMA, KNN, Random Forest) are within a few MW of each other, so there is no clear winner.
  • Seven days ahead, the simple models barely beat "same weekday last week".
  • The dates in the file look offset from the real calendar (see section 2), so holidays could not be used and the names of weekdays are only labels.
  • No weather data was used. Temperature is probably the next big gain.
